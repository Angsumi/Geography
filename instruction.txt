Act as a **senior product designer, learning-experience designer, UX/UI designer, and frontend architect** specializing in interactive education platforms such as Brilliant.org.

## Project Goal

Transform my existing website into a **mobile-first, interactive learning platform for Geography**, specifically designed around **Assam-focused competitive examinations**.

The goal is **not to make it look like a conventional coaching website, PDF notes website, or LMS**.

I want the learning experience to feel closer to **Brilliant.org**: interactive, visual, engaging, minimal, intuitive, and designed around *learning by doing* rather than passive reading.

## Existing Website

I have already built the website and it currently contains:

* Interactive CSS components
* Geography learning content
* Images and visual explanations
* Practice questions
* Interactive elements
* Existing page structure and functionality

**Do not unnecessarily rebuild everything from scratch.** First understand the existing structure, components, content, and interactions. Improve and restructure what already exists where possible.

## Core Design Philosophy

The platform should follow these principles:

### 1. Mobile First

Design for smartphones first, then progressively enhance for tablets and desktops.

Prioritize:

* One-handed usability
* Large, comfortable touch targets
* Minimal navigation
* Short content sections
* Swipe-friendly interactions where appropriate
* Fast loading
* Responsive images
* Minimal visual clutter
* Clear hierarchy

The mobile experience should feel like a **learning app**, even though it is a website.

### 2. Learning Before Information

Do not simply present Geography notes.

Turn information into **interactive learning experiences**.

Instead of:

> "The Brahmaputra enters Assam through..."

Prefer experiences such as:

> "Can you identify where the Brahmaputra enters Assam?"

Then reveal the answer through an interactive map or visual explanation.

Use:

* Interactive maps
* Clickable regions
* Image-based questions
* Drag-and-drop where useful
* MCQs
* Match-the-following
* Ordering activities
* Hotspots
* Progressive reveals
* Visual comparisons
* Mini simulations
* Timelines
* Before/after interactions
* Immediate feedback
* Short challenges

Every interaction should have a **learning purpose**. Avoid adding animation or gamification merely for decoration.

### 3. Progressive Learning

Break large topics into small learning units.

For example:

**Physical Geography of Assam**

→ Location
→ Physiographic divisions
→ Brahmaputra Valley
→ Barak Valley
→ Hills
→ Rivers
→ Floods
→ Climate
→ Soil
→ Forests
→ Resources

Each lesson should feel achievable in a few minutes.

Use a structure such as:

**Learn → Interact → Practice → Review**

### 4. Assam Exam Perspective

The platform must be specifically optimized for examinations relevant to Assam, such as:

* ADRE
* APSC
* Assam government recruitment examinations
* Other Assam-focused competitive examinations

Clearly distinguish between:

**Conceptual knowledge**

and

**Exam-oriented knowledge**

For example:

> Learn why Assam experiences flooding.

Then:

> **Exam Check:** Which river is known as the "Sorrow of Assam"?

This allows the user to understand the concept first and then convert it into exam-ready knowledge.

## UX Structure

Create a simple learning flow such as:

**Home → Subject → Topic → Lesson → Interaction → Practice → Result → Review**

Avoid complicated dashboards.

The user should always know:

1. Where am I?
2. What am I learning?
3. What should I do next?
4. How well am I doing?

## Interface Design

Aim for a **minimal, premium educational interface**.

Avoid:

* Excessive gradients
* Excessive shadows
* Crowded cards
* Too many colors
* Decorative animations
* Huge hero sections
* Corporate SaaS-style dashboards
* Traditional coaching-site aesthetics
* Information overload

Prefer:

* Strong typography
* Generous whitespace
* Clear visual hierarchy
* Simple cards
* Subtle borders
* Restrained color palette
* Consistent spacing
* Smooth micro-interactions
* High-quality maps and illustrations
* Clear feedback states

The interface should feel **calm and intelligent**, not noisy or gamified.

## Interaction Design

Every interactive component should answer:

> **"What does the student learn by interacting with this?"**

Use interaction patterns such as:

### Explore

Allow students to explore Assam geographically.

Example:

**Tap a district → see its location, rivers, geography, important facts and exam relevance.**

### Predict

Ask a question before revealing the explanation.

Example:

> "Which region of Assam is more vulnerable to annual flooding?"

Let the student choose first.

Then explain why.

### Identify

Show a map/image and ask the student to identify:

* River
* District
* Hill range
* National park
* Valley
* Border
* Geographic feature

### Compare

Allow students to compare:

* Brahmaputra Valley vs Barak Valley
* Upper Assam vs Lower Assam
* Hills vs Plains
* Different rivers
* Different climatic regions

### Practice

After learning a concept, immediately test it.

Avoid separating "study" and "practice" too far apart.

## Feedback

Feedback should teach, not merely say:

> Correct / Incorrect.

Instead:

**Correct**

> Good. The Brahmaputra flows east-to-west across much of Assam before turning south toward Bangladesh.

**Incorrect**

> Not quite. Look at the direction of the river in the map. Notice how...

Use explanations to reinforce the underlying concept.

## Gamification

Use **lightweight, meaningful gamification**.

Possible elements:

* Progress
* Streaks
* Topic completion
* XP
* Mastery levels
* Daily challenges
* Accuracy
* Weak-topic identification

Do not turn the platform into a children's game.

The target audience is **serious competitive-exam aspirants**.

But initially optimize the product around:

> **Assam Geography**

Possible main sections:

* Learn

## Lesson Architecture

Design a reusable lesson component system.

A lesson could follow:

**1. Hook**

A question, image, map, or surprising fact.

↓

**2. Concept**

Short explanation.

↓

**3. Interactive visualization**

Map / image / diagram / interaction.

↓

**4. Check your understanding**

One or two questions.

↓

**5. Exam connection**

Important competitive-exam facts.

↓

**6. Practice**

A few progressively difficult questions.

↓

**7. Quick recap**

3–5 key takeaways.

↓

**8. Mastery**

Show whether the learner has understood the topic.

## Content Presentation

Avoid long paragraphs.

Convert information into:

* Short explanations
* Visual diagrams
* Maps
* Interactive elements
* Comparison tables
* Highlighted facts
* Question prompts
* Progressive disclosure

Important exam facts should be visually distinguishable without overwhelming the page.

## Responsive Design

The website must work beautifully across:

* Small smartphones
* Large smartphones
* Tablets
* Laptops
* Large desktop screens

Do not simply shrink the desktop layout.

**Design the mobile experience first.**

Then adapt the interaction and layout for larger screens.

## Performance

Prioritize:

* Fast initial load
* Lazy-loaded images
* Optimized assets
* Minimal JavaScript
* Efficient CSS
* Responsive images
* No unnecessary libraries
* Smooth interactions on low-end Android devices

Assume a significant portion of users may have **budget Android phones and average mobile internet connections**.

## Accessibility

Follow good accessibility practices:

* Adequate contrast
* Large touch targets
* Keyboard accessibility
* Semantic HTML
* Screen-reader-friendly labels
* Avoid relying only on color
* Clear focus states
* Reduced-motion support
